# Research

<p class="lead">My research connects clinical questions in osteoarthritis and inflammatory rheumatic diseases with methods from epidemiology, machine learning and computer engineering.</p>

## Prediction in osteoarthritis and inflammatory rheumatic diseases
{: #prediction}

I develop and evaluate models to predict disease onset, progression, remission and treatment outcomes in osteoarthritis and inflammatory rheumatic diseases. A central question is whether a model remains useful when applied to people from another cohort or healthcare setting.

Published work includes prediction of remission in rheumatoid arthritis through the JAK-pot collaboration, prediction of rheumatoid arthritis in first-degree relatives, and prediction of end-stage knee osteoarthritis. Current research extends to drug-free remission, disease trajectories and models that combine clinical information with knowledge about the disease and its environment.

## Osteoarthritis: risk factors and disease progression
{: #osteoarthritis}

I investigate how pain, structural changes and functional limitations evolve in knee, hip and hand osteoarthritis. I study how these outcomes relate to one another over time and across joints, and investigate clinical, environmental and biological factors associated with disease risk and progression.

My projects examine body weight, environmental exposures, metabolic profiles and medication use, alongside symptom and structural progression. Analyses draw on longitudinal cohorts including the Osteoarthritis Initiative (OAI), the Multicenter Osteoarthritis Study (MOST) and CHECK.

## Environment, metabolism and the microbiome
{: #environment}

I study environmental and biological factors that may help explain differences in disease risk and progression. These include per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), metabolomic profiles, the microbiome and nutritional factors.

Projects cover environmental exposures and osteoarthritis, metabolomics in joint disease, and microbiome-related research in rheumatoid arthritis. Through the INTEGRAAL doctoral programme, I also co-supervise work connecting the exposome, disease knowledge and prediction.

## Treatment outcomes and population health
{: #treatments}

I use clinical registries and routinely collected health data to investigate treatment outcomes, safety and multimorbidity. My methodological work includes target trial emulation, which uses observational data to address a question framed as a hypothetical trial.

Research spans rheumatoid arthritis, systemic sclerosis and vasculitis. Work using the French National Health Data System (SNDS) includes SELF-MAITS, a study of links between mental health disorders, autoimmune diseases and their treatments, alongside projects on disease trajectories and treatment outcomes.

## Digital twins and clinical AI
{: #digital-twins}

I work on methods for connecting patient data with computational models that can be updated over time. This includes defining what a healthcare digital twin should do, how its predictions should be evaluated and what evidence is needed before clinical use.

In the ANR-funded MaChroTwin project, I lead work on computational modelling and cardiovascular digital twin integration. Related projects examine AI methods for clinical information and the role of AI in medical uncertainty.

## Methods across these themes

Across projects, I use longitudinal modelling, machine learning, evidence synthesis and approaches to causal inference. I place particular emphasis on external validation, appropriate handling of continuous variables, and reproducible analysis workflows.
